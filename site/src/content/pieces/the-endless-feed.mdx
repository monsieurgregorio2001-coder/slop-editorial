A decade ago a publication had a budget for images, and the budget did the first round of editing. Every picture that ran had been paid for, which meant someone had already said yes to it.

Generation removed that filter. A model can produce a thousand plausible covers before lunch. Nothing in the process asks whether any of them should exist.

<Note mode="note" label="Working definition">
Slop is not bad content. Slop is content nobody decided to make.
</Note>

## What replaces the budget

The old constraint was money, and it worked because it was external. The replacement has to be deliberate. A good studio now writes down its rules before it generates anything. What the series is about. What it refuses to show. Who signs off.

Those rules are not creative limits. They are the editor, expressed in advance, so that the machine has something to be measured against.

## Where the effort goes

Most of the work moves to the end of the pipeline. Generating is quick, so the hours go into comparing candidates, rejecting near misses, and explaining the choice in a sentence. If the reason cannot be said plainly, the image probably should not run.
